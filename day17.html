Section 2

Reading

Video (watch) · Exercise (hands-on)

Both directors start from one line. The difference is what kind of line.

Vanga

Deep on one truth

The line is an emotion: a son acts like an animal for his father. Every scene grows outward from that feeling, pushing it further and further.

Rajamouli

Wide on one world

The line is a situation: the rightful heir returns. Around it: love, betrayal, politics, spectacle, a kingdom of threads.

The question for every film you make: one feeling stretched thin, or one world with many people? A 2-minute philosophy short is almost always Vanga-shaped: one feeling, deep.

Your shadow opening

Your idea: two shadows in frame, recorded voices, a rotating camera angle, no faces, no full scenes. A camera move or a sound marks each pause. It began with an elder man in a village; slowly two characters and their speech appear, and the dialogue leads the audience into a thought area. Then you decide whether that happens in a village or wherever the conflict suggests.

Three principles from the call:

  1. Dialogue before environment. Write what they say before you decide where they are.
  2. Motivation doesn't need a location. Shadows can carry two wants on a blank wall.
  3. Every camera move or sound in a pause should mean something. And rotation needs a reason (power shift, a thought turning, time passing), or it's a gimmick.
Worked example

A shadow opening, sketched (an example, not the answer)

Wants: the elder wants the young man to stay in the village; the young man wants permission to leave. Thought to hold: is staying loyalty or fear? Place: nowhere specific, just a lamp-lit wall.

  • Shot 1: two shadows on a wall, static. Voices only. Ambience: night insects (bed), a lamp hissing (middle), a breath (close).
  • Pause 1: silence, then a slow quarter-rotation as the young man's voice gets stronger. Reason: power shifting.
  • Pause 2: the lamp flickers; no move. Reason: time passing.
  • End: the elder's shadow is now the smaller one. Silence.
Section 3

Exercise (hands-on)

Video (watch) · Reading

  1. Decide: is The Experience Machine a Vanga film (one feeling, deep) or a Rajamouli film (one world, wide)? Write two sentences.
  2. Answer your three open questions for the shadow opening: What does each shadow character want from the other? What thought should the audience be left holding at the end? Village, jail, school, or nowhere specific?
  3. Write 6-10 lines of dialogue first (dialogue before environment).
  4. Mark every pause in the dialogue. For each, choose a camera move or a sound, and write its reason.
  5. Record the two voices on your phone (yours, or a friend's), and lay them over a black screen in CapCut with your pause sounds.
What good looks like
  • A clear Vanga-or-Rajamouli call with a reason.
  • Two wants that can't both win, and one thought for the audience in a single sentence.
  • Every rotation has a written reason: power, thought, or time.
  • A voice-plus-sound sketch where the pauses feel meaningful even with no picture.
