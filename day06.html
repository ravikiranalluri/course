Section 2

Reading

Video (watch) · Exercise (hands-on)

Your rule: no music. Instead, three layers plus silence. This forces every sound to mean something, which is exactly what a director wants.

  1. Ambience bed: the room or the world. Desert wind, a fridge hum, distant traffic. It tells us where we are. Never fully off unless you mean it.
  2. Middle layer: actions. Footsteps, knife on board, a door. It tells us what's happening.
  3. Close detail: intimate sounds. Hair brushing a cheek, a breath, a ring tapping wood. It tells us how she feels.
  4. Silence: when the layers drop away. It tells us this moment matters. Real silence is rare, so it's powerful.

Section 3

Exercise (hands-on)

Video (watch) · Reading

  1. Sit in your kitchen with eyes closed for 2 minutes. Write every sound you hear, sorted into ambience, middle and close.
  2. Record 30 seconds of each layer on your phone (room tone, chopping, a close breath or a ring tapping).
  3. In CapCut, stack the three recordings, then cut all of them to silence for 1 second at the end.
  4. Listen back with headphones. Write one line on what the silence did.
What good looks like
  • At least 3 sounds per layer in your list.
  • Three clean recordings, each clearly one layer (not everything at once).
  • The silence feels like an event, not like a mistake. If it feels like a mistake, the layers before it were too quiet; raise them.
