Section 2

Reading

Video (watch) · Exercise (hands-on)

A first film needs a topic you can picture, not just explain. The Experience Machine wins because it already contains a scene: someone plugged into a perfect life, and a reason to unplug.

The thought experiment, simply: a machine could give you any experience you wanted, forever, and it would feel completely real. Would you plug in? Most people say no. Nozick's reasons: we want to actually do things, not just feel we did; we want to be a certain kind of person; and we want contact with reality.

Your hook: A machine can give you a perfect life forever, but none of it would be real. Most people say no. Here's why that's strange.

Notice the hook doesn't explain. It promises a question. That's what keeps someone watching past 10 seconds.

Worked example

From topic to scene

Topic: the experience machine. Picture: a woman on a perfect sunset road; a woman in a real kitchen with a knife and her husband's keys. Question: which one is real? Twist options: the road is the machine, the road is a memory, or the kitchen is the machine. You'll pick one later (Day 24). For now, the topic has become two images and a question. That's a film.

Section 3

Exercise (hands-on)

Video (watch) · Reading

  1. Write 20 philosophy topics you'd love to film (thought experiments, paradoxes, old questions). Don't judge yet.
  2. Next to each, write one image you can picture. Cross out any you can't picture.
  3. From what's left, rank your top 3 by: can you picture it, can you make it with AI, would you watch it.
  4. Confirm The Experience Machine (or argue against it on paper). Write its hook in your own words.
What good looks like
  • 20 topics, at least 8 with a clear image.
  • Your top 3 each have one picture-able scene, not just a concept.
  • Your hook is one or two sentences, makes a promise and asks a question.
