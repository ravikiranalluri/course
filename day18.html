Section 2

Reading

Video (watch) · Exercise (hands-on)

Structure first, trailer craft later. You'll skip studying trailers for now. A 10-30 second trailer or reel is a compression of a story, and you can't compress what you can't structure. Trailers come in Week 10.

So today is a full-film watch, slowly, with the Movie Watch Sheet: Movie Watch Sheet (PDF) · PNG. Three columns:

  • Want: what does the person on screen want right now? Who wants the opposite?
  • Color: what's the palette, and what feeling does it carry? (Arrakis gold, Giedi Prime's black-and-white sun.)
  • Broken Rules: where does the film break a "rule" you've learned (centered framing, huge negative space, a held shot that's too long), and why does it work?

Watch the horizon. Greig Fraser places the horizon very deliberately: often extremely low, with tiny figures under vast sky, or pushed high to bury people in sand. Compare it to your Day 13 road rule (low horizon = exposed).

Worked example

One row of the sheet, filled in

~Time: the first worm ride (approximate; note your own). Want: Paul wants to be accepted by the Fremen; the desert (the worm) "wants" to throw him. Color: orange-gold sand, pale sky, dust. Broken Rules: very wide shots with a tiny figure during the most personal moment. The size breaks the "go close for emotion" rule, and it works because the emotion is scale itself.

Section 3

Exercise (hands-on)

Video (watch) · Reading

  1. Print the Movie Watch Sheet (or copy its three columns into your notebook).
  2. Watch Dune: Part Two from the start. Pause every 5 minutes.
  3. At each pause, write one line per column (Want, Color, Broken Rules) and do a 30-second thumbnail sketch of the frame, marking where the horizon sits.
  4. Slow down for the three focus scenes: the first sandworm ride, the Giedi Prime arena, the final duel. Fill extra rows for each.
  5. At the end, circle the three rows that taught you the most.
What good looks like
  • Roughly 30 rows across the film, each with all three columns.
  • Thumbnails with horizon lines drawn. You start noticing patterns (low for awe, high for being trapped).
  • At least five Broken Rules with a reason why each works.
  • Three circled rows you can talk about for a minute each.
