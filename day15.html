Section 2

Reading

Video (watch) · Exercise (hands-on)

Beginners invent a cool location first ("a lighthouse!") and then try to put a story in it. Directors work the other way:

Don't invent the situation on top of the wants. Let the two wants bump into each other and the situation shows up on its own.

The method: write two people's wants so that they can't both win. Then ask: where would these two wants actually meet? What time is it? What's in their hands? The place, time and props fall out of the collision.

Worked example

Three seeds, grown into situations

Want AWant BThe situation that falls out
A young woman wants her father's approvalHer father wants to protect her from his own pastKitchen at midnight. He's washing dishes (busy hands, back turned, avoiding her eyes); she's in the doorway (not fully in, not out).
A thief wants one more chance from the man he betrayedThat man wants to believe him but can't afford toA car at a red light. Trapped together, a ticking deadline (the light will change), neither looking at the other.
A soldier wants to confess before dawnHer commander wants to stop herA tent, a lantern, a countdown. Dawn is the deadline; the lantern is the only light, and it can be put out.

Notice: nobody chose "kitchen" because kitchens look nice. Midnight dishes is what a man avoiding his daughter would actually be doing. The props (dishes, the doorway) are the wants made physical.

Section 3

Exercise (hands-on)

Video (watch) · Reading

  1. Pick one of the three seeds above. Without looking at the given situation, write three different situations where those two wants could meet.
  2. For each, list place, time and two props. Circle the one where the props carry the wants.
  3. Write your own seed for The Experience Machine: what does Mara want, and who (or what) wants the opposite? (Hint: the husband who left the keys, or the machine itself.)
  4. Let the situation fall out: where and when do those two wants meet? Write it in three lines.
What good looks like
  • Each situation has place, time and props, and you can say which want each prop belongs to.
  • Your chosen situation creates a deadline or a trap (a light about to change, dawn coming).
  • Your Experience Machine seed has two wants that can't both win, and the kitchen (or a new place) falls out of them rather than being assumed.
