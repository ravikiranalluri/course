Section 2

Reading

Video (watch) · Exercise (hands-on)

A camera move should feel like a thought. If the camera moves, something changed: attention, feeling, or knowledge.

  1. Static (locked-off): the world moves, the camera doesn't. Calm, observation, or held tension.
  2. Pan / tilt: the camera turns on the spot. Following, or revealing what's beside or above.
  3. Push in / pull out (dolly): moving closer means "pay attention, this matters"; moving away means "leave her alone" or "see the bigger picture".
  4. Track: moving alongside. We walk with her; we're a companion.
  5. Crane: rising or descending. Rising reveals scale or fate; descending lands us in a moment.

The dolly zoom combines a pull-out with a zoom-in. The subject stays the same size; the background warps. It's the feeling of the floor dropping away. Spielberg uses it at the exact second Brody realizes the attack is happening.

Write moves precisely for AI: "slow dolly push from medium shot to close-up over 8 seconds", not "cinematic camera".

Worked example

Moves for The Experience Machine

  • Road: static, or a very slow push. She walks toward us; the camera barely moves, so she enters our space.
  • Face: static. The finger clearing her hair is the motion; the camera stays out of its way.
  • Kitchen: static during the cutting; maybe a tiny push-in during the pause on the keys: "this is the decision".

Notice: almost no movement. A beginner wants to move the camera constantly. A director moves it only when the meaning moves.

Section 3

Exercise (hands-on)

Video (watch) · Reading

  1. In Blender (or with toy objects on a table and your phone), set up a grey box: a table, a cube for the person, a small block for the keys.
  2. Shoot each of the five moves, 5 seconds each.
  3. Try a homemade dolly zoom: walk backward while zooming in on the phone. Keep the cube the same size.
  4. Watch all six clips. Next to each, write the feeling in one word, and which moment of the kitchen scene it could serve.
What good looks like
  • Six clips, each one move only (no mixed moves).
  • Your dolly zoom keeps the subject roughly the same size while the background shifts.
  • Each clip has a feeling word and a scene moment. If you can't name a moment, that move isn't needed in this film, which is useful to know.
