The cold-question exercise

Short questions, answered without overthinking. Each answer is recorded as a data point. When there are enough data points, you draft the shot, scene or script from them instead of from a blank page.

Today’s call, in order

Sat Oct 24, 2026, from 11:34 PM ET. This call only, as close to verbatim as the call passed it on.

Q1

When you picture the Utah road shot, what’s the feeling?

First “freedom,” then you corrected it yourself to isolation: immersed in the self, walking on an empty road that shows light. It’s not literally about an empty highway.

Q2

What is the light doing? Is it there to reveal her?

The light is the subject itself, not there to reveal the character. She sits in a golden triangle at the center, with sunset golden lighting wrapping around her, a pleasant gold, not flat.

Q3

Which way is she walking, and why?

Toward the camera. She’s walking into the viewer’s space to pull them into what comes next. Later shots explain what she’s going through.

Q4

What do we hear?

No music. Just the slow summer evening sunset wind from the desert’s temperature difference, a gentle breeze. If it were gentler, her hair would fly, so a close-up on her face with her hair slightly covering it. To reveal her beauty as a heroine entrance, she clears the hair from her face with her index finger, slowly.

Your self-assessment and feedback

  • You corrected “freedom” to “isolation” on your own. That’s a director sharpening the feeling instead of settling for the first word.
  • You kept adding detail (wind, hair, finger, light) without losing the thread. Every new detail served the same feeling.
  • You were picturing, not performing: you described what you saw in your head, not what sounded impressive. Keep doing exactly that.

Shot notes drawn from these answers

  • Feeling: isolation, turned inward.
  • Subject: the light; the road and sky carry it.
  • Composition: woman centered, inside a golden triangle formed by the road and the sunset light; wide sky, road as the only hard edge.
  • Light: sunset gold wrapping around her, warm and pleasant, not harsh.
  • Move: she walks toward the lens; it works as an opening that hands the viewer into the next shots.
