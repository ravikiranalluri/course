Section 2

Reading

Video (watch) · Exercise (hands-on)

Start from what she wants. Your reminder: lean on the character, hold the want in one line, and let emotion, conflict and theme grow out of it.

The keys moment hands you the want. Her husband's forgotten car keys sit on the table. She hasn't decided whether to take them to him. So the want is about staying or going: "She wants the evening to stay perfect, and to be left alone inside it." That's isolation turned into something a person does.

Collect before you create: five examples of the feeling, each with one line on what it does to you. Paris, Texas (a man shrunk by a desert), Lost in Translation (a window over a city she can't touch), Nomadland (a woman small against open land), The Truman Show (a perfect place that's a trap), Up (a life in a wordless montage).

A color script is a strip of colored boxes, one per beat, so you can see the feeling change before you make a single shot. Pixar draws one for every film.

Worked example

The 6-beat color script of the 10-second opening

1 open blue
2 road gold
3 warm face
4 gold in walls
5 near-miss
6 still pause
  1. Open blue sky over the road: space, exposure.
  2. Road gold: sunset as the subject.
  3. Warm gold on her face: we meet her.
  4. Gold coming through the kitchen window: the same light, now inside walls.
  5. Darker amber at the near-miss: shock.
  6. Deep, quiet shadow at the pause: she's alone with the keys.

The light travels from outside to inside, and the color darkens as she turns inward. That's the emotional arc you described, as color.

Section 3

Exercise (hands-on)

Video (watch) · Reading

  1. Write her want in one line. Test it: does the keys moment threaten it? If not, rewrite.
  2. Write your five isolation examples with one line each on what they do to you.
  3. In CapCut, make six solid-color clips (use the color script above or your own), about 1.5 seconds each.
  4. Add your three sound layers under them (breeze, closer breeze, knife rhythm, then silence). No music.
  5. Watch it with eyes half-closed. Does the feeling move from open to inward?
What good looks like
  • A want line under 15 words that the keys directly threaten.
  • Five examples, each with a line about your reaction, not a plot summary.
  • A 9-10 second CapCut sketch where color and sound change together, ending in silence and darkness.
  • Someone who knows nothing about the film can say 'it gets more alone' after watching it.
