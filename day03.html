Section 2

Reading

Video (watch) · Exercise (hands-on)

Directors learn by stealing structure, not shots. A breakdown is just a set of questions you ask the same way every time, so the answers become comparable data points.

The director's checklist (use it for every reference):

  1. Promise: what does the title plus the first 10 seconds promise me?
  2. Camera eye: shot size, angle and lens feel at three key moments (a wide, a medium, a close or unusual one).
  3. Drop risk: where would I stop watching? (A slow explanation, a soft visual, a lull.)
  4. Steal: the one prompt I'd give an AI tool or Blender to recreate the strongest shot.
  5. Judge: keep, cut or restyle, if this were my channel?
Worked example

What Remains, first pass (how a filled-in row reads)

Promise: an emotional sci-fi short, no lecture. Camera eye: opens wide and quiet, moves to close faces quickly, holds silences. Drop risk: any stretch where we look at scenery without a person. Steal: "slow dolly push from medium shot to tight close-up over 8 seconds, warm window light". Judge: keep the restraint; restyle toward your sunset-gold look.

This is a model of the format, not a verdict on the film. Your own answers are what count.

Section 3

Exercise (hands-on)

Video (watch) · Reading

  1. Watch the three videos above. Keep a notebook open.
  2. Fill the five checklist questions for each video (15 answers total).
  3. Circle the one 'Steal' that fits The Experience Machine best.
  4. Write one line: what will your film promise in its first 10 seconds?
What good looks like
  • 15 short answers, each specific enough that someone else could find the moment (include rough times).
  • At least one drop-risk moment named per video. Every film has one.
  • Your promise line names a feeling and a question, for example 'a woman alone in perfect light; is any of it real?'
