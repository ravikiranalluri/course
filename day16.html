Section 2

Reading

Video (watch) · Exercise (hands-on)

This is the best long dialogue scene to study for wants in collision. Landa wants to find the hidden Jewish family. LaPadite wants to protect them. Neither says it. Every sip, pause and pipe says it.

BeatWhat happensWho holds power
1 ArrivalCars approach across the field; LaPadite's family is sent inside.Landa, from a distance
2 The milkLanda asks for milk and drinks it slowly. Politeness as pressure.Landa, disguised as a guest
3 Interrogation and pipesThe questions narrow; Landa's comically large pipe against LaPadite's plain one.Shifting toward Landa
4 The floor reveal (~halfway)The camera sinks below the floorboards to show the family hiding underneath.We now know what's at stake
5 Hawk vs ratLanda's speech; his close-up; LaPadite breaks.Landa, completely
6 Through the floorSoldiers shoot through the floor; Shosanna escapes across the field.Landa, but one want survives

The wants clash hardest from the milk to the floor reveal. Once we see the family, every polite line becomes unbearable.

The arc with a reason

Tarantino arcs the camera around the two men at the table, and their screen sides swap as power shifts. Before, LaPadite holds his side of the frame; after, the composition belongs to Landa. The movement isn't decoration; it is the power changing hands.

Worked example

Why this matters for your shadow shot

Your shadow opening idea uses a rotating camera. This scene is the model: rotate when power shifts, when a thought turns, or when time passes. If the camera rotates and nothing changes between the two shadows, it's a gimmick. If it rotates exactly as one voice gains the upper hand, it's direction.

Section 3

Exercise (hands-on)

Video (watch) · Reading

  1. Watch your copy from ~1:52 to ~20:00 once without stopping.
  2. Watch again. Fill the beat table above in your own words, with your own rough timestamps.
  3. Mark the exact moment you feel power change hands. What is the camera doing at that moment?
  4. Draw a top-down floor plan of the table: two dots for the men, an arrow for the camera's arc, and note where each man sits on screen before and after.
  5. Write one sentence: when would the camera rotate in your shadow opening, and what would have changed?
What good looks like
  • Six beats with your own timestamps, marked approximate.
  • A power-shift moment you can point to, with what the camera does there.
  • A floor plan showing the arc and the side swap.
  • A rotation rule for your shadow shot that names a reason (power, thought, or time), not just 'it looks cool'.
