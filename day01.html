The nine shot sizes, and how distance equals relationship.

Section 2

Reading

Video (watch) · Exercise (hands-on)

Every shot answers one question before anything else: how close are we to this person? Distance is relationship. Far away, we observe a person inside a world. Close up, we are inside their feeling.

SizeWhat fills the frameWhat it tells the viewer
Extreme wide (EWS)Landscape, person tiny or absentWhere we are; scale; isolation
Wide (WS)Whole body plus surroundingsWhat she is doing, in what place
Full / medium wideHead to feet / kneesBody language
Medium (MS)Waist upConversation distance; neutral
Medium close (MCU)Chest upAttention on the face, still some context
Close-up (CU)FaceEmotion, thought
Extreme close-up (ECU)Eyes, a hand, an objectThe one detail that matters right now
InsertAn objectA clue or a decision (the keys)
Two-shotTwo peopleThe relationship itself

Rule of thumb: change size when the feeling changes, not because you're bored. A cut from wide to close is the film saying "now look closer".

Worked example

The Experience Machine opening, by size

  • Seconds 1-4, the road: extreme wide. A lone figure far down the road under a whole sky. Size says: she is small, alone, the world is huge. That is the isolation feeling.
  • Seconds 5-7, her face: close-up. We jump from world to person. The size change is the turn of the story.
  • Seconds 8-10, the kitchen: medium side angle on her hands and the knife, then an insert of the car keys. Close interior after open exterior.

Three sizes, three feelings, ten seconds. Nothing else has been decided yet, and the opening already has a shape.

Section 3

Exercise (hands-on)

Video (watch) · Reading

  1. Take three frames from the Worlds Before Us video (screenshot at 0:10, 0:45, 1:30) and label each one's shot size.
  2. On paper, draw nine small rectangles. Sketch the Utah road scene in each of the nine sizes, using stick figures.
  3. Circle the one size that best says 'isolation' for the road. Write one line on why.
  4. Do the same for the kitchen: which size best shows the near-miss with the knife?
What good looks like
  • Every frame is labeled with the right size, and you can say what each one tells the viewer.
  • Your road choice is a wide or extreme wide, with a reason tied to the feeling, not to 'it looks cool'.
  • Your kitchen choice is close enough to see the knife and fingers.
