Until now you designed light, angle and move separately. A director designs them together, per beat, because they answer the same question: what should she feel right now?

The shot design table keeps them in one place:

Worked example

The opening, designed as one

Seconds8-10

The kitchen

Size
Medium side, then insert on keys

Eye level, then overhead
Lens
50mm, then 85mm insert
Light

Static, tiny push on the keys

Knife rhythm, near-miss, silence

Read down any column and it tells a story too: wide to close, hard light to warm wrap to window beam, breeze to silence.

Section 3

Exercise (hands-on)

Video (watch) · Reading

  1. Copy the shot design table. Change at least one choice per beat so it's yours, and note why.
  2. Make a 10-second Blender previs: three grey-box shots with the camera moves and timings from your table.
  3. Pick the kitchen shot (8-10s). Upload its previs to Wan 3.0 video-to-video with a prompt that keeps camera and motion but re-renders the look (warm sunset kitchen, woman in apron, wooden table, keys).
  4. Judge the result against your table: what matched, what drifted, one prompt change for next time.
What good looks like
  • A table where every beat has size, angle, lens, light, move and sound filled.
  • A previs that runs about 10 seconds with three clear beats.
  • A Wan output that keeps your camera move. Imperfect is fine; you're testing the pipeline.
  • A written judgment with one specific fix, for example 'add focus locked on the keys from first frame to last'.
