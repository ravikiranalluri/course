Section 2

Reading

Video (watch) · Exercise (hands-on)

Size says how close. Angle says who has the power.

  • Eye level: equal. We meet the person as a peer. The default for honesty.
  • Low angle (camera below, looking up): the subject grows. Power, threat, heroism.
  • High angle (camera above, looking down): the subject shrinks. Vulnerable, watched, small.
  • Overhead / top-down: a god's view, or a map. Pattern, fate, a moment of decision seen from outside.
  • Dutch (tilted): the world is off balance. Use rarely; it's loud.

The trick: the audience doesn't notice angles consciously. They just feel bigger or smaller next to the character.

Worked example

Angles in the kitchen

Same moment, the near-miss with the knife, three angles:

  • Eye level, side: we're beside her, a neutral witness. It feels like real life (the version in the draft).
  • Overhead on the cutting board: her hands, the knife, the keys at the edge of frame. Now it's about the decision. We see the whole board like a chessboard.
  • Low, from the table: she towers, knife blurred in the foreground. It becomes threatening, which is wrong for this feeling.

Judgment: eye level for the cutting, overhead for the pause with the keys. The angle changes when the meaning changes.

Section 3

Exercise (hands-on)

Video (watch) · Reading

  1. Stand in your own kitchen with your phone. Shoot the same object (a knife next to keys) at eye level, low, high, overhead and Dutch.
  2. Put the five photos side by side. Under each, write one word for the feeling.
  3. Pick the angle for the 'keys on the table' moment in The Experience Machine, and write one sentence on why.
What good looks like
  • Five photos of the same object, clearly different heights or tilts.
  • Your feeling words differ (for example 'neutral, threat, small, decision, unease'). If two are the same word, look again.
  • Your choice for the keys is tied to what she is deciding, not to a pretty picture.
