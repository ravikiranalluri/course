Section 2

Reading

Video (watch) · Exercise (hands-on)

Your road look, in five ingredients:

  1. Low horizon: two-thirds or more of the frame is sky. Exposed, open, nowhere to hide.
  2. One hard line: the road is the only hard edge. No hills, no trees, no poles. Simplicity makes her the only story.
  3. Low, hard sun: long shadows stretching toward camera, a rim on her hair. Hard light reads clearly different from overcast or glass.
  4. Sunset gradient: blue at the top of frame melting into gold and ember near the horizon.
  5. Heat shimmer: the air above the asphalt wobbles. It makes the far figure slightly unreal, which is great for a twist where the road might be the machine.
Worked example

A prompt built from the five ingredients

"Extreme wide shot, 35mm, locked-off camera at knee height. An empty straight paved desert road in Utah running to a low horizon; the sky fills the top three-quarters of the frame, deep blue at the top fading to gold and ember at the horizon. No hills, no trees, no poles; the road is the only hard line. Low hard sunset sun behind the camera's left, long shadows. A lone woman far down the road walking toward camera, small, centered. Heat shimmer above the asphalt. No text."

Every phrase maps to an ingredient. If a generation adds mountains, the "one hard line" rule failed; add "flat featureless horizon" and regenerate.

Section 3

Exercise (hands-on)

Video (watch) · Reading

  1. Write your own road prompt using the five ingredients. Generate 4 stills.
  2. Score each still 0-2 on each ingredient (max 10).
  3. Fix the weakest ingredient with one prompt change and regenerate.
  4. Put your best road still next to your kitchen still from Day 11. Write one line on the contrast (open sky vs closed interior).
What good looks like
  • A still scoring 8+ out of 10 on your own checklist.
  • You changed one thing at a time and can say what improved.
  • The road and kitchen stills feel like two halves of one feeling: open outward, then turned inward.
